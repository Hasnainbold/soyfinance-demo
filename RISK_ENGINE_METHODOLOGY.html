The one thing to say first

Framing that survives scrutiny

"The score is an ordinal risk index (0–100), not a probability of default. The quantitative methods inside it — Value-at-Risk, volatility, the Cuddy–Della Valle instability index, the grain-shrink formula — are established and citable. The pillar weights and scoring thresholds are expert-set priors that we will calibrate against our own repayment data as the book builds. Every score ships with a data-provenance breakdown so you can see how much of it rests on verified inputs."

That framing holds up in a due-diligence room. “Our model predicts default probability” does not — not until it has been fitted and backtested against realised outcomes, which requires a closed-loan history the platform does not yet have.

How to read the classification

Every factor below carries one of four tags. The point of separating them is credibility: naming what is not yet proven is what makes the claims about what is proven believable.

  • ①Established method

    Peer-reviewed or textbook-standard technique. Citable. Not invented here.

  • ②Standard practice

    How secured & commodity lenders actually operate, or a factually correct feature of the Indian soybean market.

  • ③Calibrated heuristic

    The metric is sound; the breakpoints are informed by published ranges; the points assigned are our scoring curve.

  • ④Design choice

    Our expert judgement. Reasonable, but must be recalibrated against realised loan outcomes.

The six pillars, factor by factor

PILLAR 1

Price Level Risk

20 / 100

Is soybean cheap or expensive right now, and where is it heading?

Sub-score weights: MSP distance 30% · forecast trend 35% · 2-yr percentile 20% · change since deposit 15%
FactorBasisSource / note
MSP distance as a price-floor signal②MSP plus NAFED / state procurement demonstrably acts as a soft floor for soybean mandi prices (CACP price-policy reports). The concept is sound.
MSP breakpoints (0.95×, 1.0×, 1.1×) and points 95/75/40/15④Our scoring curve.
Forecast-trend sub-score from the ML price forecast③Using a model forecast as an input is standard; the ensemble itself is standard ML (gradient boosting + random forest + ridge). The −10% / −5% drop thresholds are ours.
Position in the 2-year price range (mean-reversion)③Percentile / z-score positioning is a standard commodity signal; the 90 / 70 / 30 cutoffs are ours.
Change since deposit price (mark-to-market vs entry)②Universal in collateral lending. Thresholds ours.
Sub-score weights 30 / 35 / 20 / 15④Design choice.

Data today: live price and 2-yr history are real (mandi API); MSP is a reference table; forecast is model-derived from real prices; deposit price comes from the receipt.

PILLAR 2

Price Volatility Risk

25 / 100 · largest

How violently does this price move, and how bad could a 90-day drawdown be? — runs entirely on the real daily price series, which makes it the most trustworthy pillar today.

annualised_vol = stdev(daily_returns over 30d) × √252
VaR₅₅(H)     = − percentile₅( Pₜ / Pₜ₋ₐ − 1 )  // 5th pct of H-day returns
CDVI         = (σ/μ × 100) × √(1 − R²)  // detrended instability
Sub-score weights: volatility 35% · regime 25% · VaR 25% · CDVI 15%
FactorBasisSource / note
Annualised rolling volatility (σ × √252)①Standard quantitative finance — √time scaling of volatility, 252 trading days. Hull, Options, Futures and Other Derivatives.
Value-at-Risk, historical simulation, 5th-percentile horizon return①One of the three canonical VaR methods. Jorion, Value at Risk; Basel market-risk framework.
Cuddy–Della Valle Instability Index, CV × √(1−R²)①Cuddy & Della Valle (1978), Oxford Bulletin of Economics and Statistics. The most India-agri-economics-credible component — this exact index runs through the Indian crop area / price / production instability literature.
Coefficient of variation, linear detrending (R²)①Elementary statistics.
Volatility regime vs the series’ own 25th / 75th percentile③Percentile-based regime classification is standard; the low / medium / high → 10 / 40 / 80 mapping is ours.
Breakpoints: vol 15/25/35%, VaR 5/10/20%, CDVI 10/20/35③Breakpoints informed by what low / medium / high commodity volatility looks like; the point values are our curve.
Sub-score weights 35 / 25 / 25 / 15④Design choice.

Known limitation to disclose: overlapping horizon returns inflate estimator autocorrelation; the √days fallback for short histories assumes i.i.d. returns (no volatility clustering). Standard simplifications a quant reviewer will note.

PILLAR 3

Warehouse & Storage Risk

15 / 100

Is the collateral actually secure where it is sitting?

Sub-score weights: WDRA + registry 30% · insurance 25% · storage duration 20% · collateral manager 15% · location 10%
FactorBasisSource / note
WDRA registration / e-NWR as a collateral-quality signal②Factually correct: WDRA-registered warehouses issue regulated negotiable warehouse receipts through the e-NWR repository; registration materially reduces fraud and quality risk.
Inspection-staleness penalty (> 180d, > 365d)④Reasonable direction; our thresholds.
Insurance: type + validity + coverage ratio (sum insured ÷ market value)②Coverage-ratio testing is standard in secured lending.
Storage-duration risk rising with time in store②Directionally correct — longer storage means more spoilage, shrinkage and price exposure.
Storage-duration breakpoints (60 / 120 / 180d) and points④Our scoring curve.
Collateral manager present → lower risk②Independent collateral management is standard practice in Indian agri-commodity finance since the 2013 NSEL episode.
Location flood-risk lookup table (Indore 20, Ujjain 30, …)weakest element④Hardcoded estimates. Replace with Central Water Commission flood-hazard / district disaster data before diligence. Flag this proactively — it is 10% of a 15%-weight pillar, i.e. 1.5% of the composite.
Sub-score weights 30 / 25 / 20 / 15 / 10④Design choice.

Data today: currently synthetic in the demo — now capturable as real via the receipt-intake form (lab slip, e-NWR number, policy).

PILLAR 4

LTV & Quality Risk

15 / 100

How much cushion between the loan and what the grain is really worth — after physical losses?

shrink%ₓₒₓₛₜ₰₨ₑ = (M_wet − M_target) / (100 − M_target) × 100  // M_target = 10%
realised_fraction(t) = 1 − 0.5 ^ (days / 30)  // ~30-day equilibration
effective_value_factor = 1 − total_loss% / 100  // discounts collateral value
Sub-score weights: initial LTV 30% · live LTV 25% · grade/quality 20% · margin-call distance 15% · shrinkage loss 10%
FactorBasisSource / note
Loan-to-value as the primary collateral-risk axis②Universal in secured lending.
LTV breakpoints (60 / 75 / 85%)③~70–80% is typical for warehouse-receipt finance by Indian banks / NBFCs; our specific bands.
Live (mark-to-market) LTV using the current price②Standard for commodity-backed lending.
Margin-call distance = (live value − loan) ÷ loan②Standard repo / commodity-finance concept. Thresholds ours.
Grain moisture-shrink formula (M_wet−M_dry)/(100−M_dry)×100①Standard grain-trade dry-matter-conservation formula. Purdue University Agronomy Extension, “Converting Wet Corn Weight to Dry Corn Weight” (cited in code). Correct math, not an approximation.
Safe-storage moisture target ~10%, high-risk > 13%②Consistent with FCI / NAFED oilseed procurement specs and extension guidance for safe soybean storage.
Moisture-equilibration half-life = 30 days④Modeling assumption for a WDRA-standard godown, not measured.
Ongoing dry-matter loss 0.15%/mo (good) – 0.35%/mo (poor)③Within the range of published post-harvest storage-loss figures (FAO; NABCONS 2015); the good / poor split is our modeling choice.
Quality specs (moisture ≤10, oil ≥19, protein ≥38, FM ≤1 = good)③Broadly consistent with fair-average-quality / soybean trade specs; exact cutoffs and scoring are ours.
Blend of coarse A/B/C grade with lab params (40 : 60)④Design choice.
The shrinkage model as a whole③The code states it: “a rule-based estimator calibrated to published storage-loss ranges, not a live prediction … should be replaced with an ML model once real weigh-in / weigh-out records are available.” Present it exactly that way.

Data today: LTV inputs from the receipt; live price real; shrinkage from the moisture + days model (rule-based).

Underwriting & verification — borrower financial data

The methodology above governs the score. This section governs its inputs: for each fact about a borrower's finances, which document establishes it and how it is checked against an independent source of truth. The test an investor applies is one question — is each number machine-verified against a registry, or self-declared?

Verification strength

A separate axis from the ①–④ tiers above. That scale rated how established a method is; this one rates how an input is confirmed.

  • ●Machine-verified

    Checked against a government, bureau or registry API. The borrower cannot fake it.

  • ●Institution-sourced

    Data arrives signed from the bank itself via the Account Aggregator framework — not a borrower upload.

  • ●Third-party verified

    An accredited external party attests: KYC vendor, NABL lab, insurer, FPO.

  • ●Self-declared + audited

    Borrower states it; sample-checked and cross-referenced. Never the sole basis for a lending number.

The loan file a diligence team will sample

For any loan picked at random, this bundle exists and every number is independently re-verifiable.

  1. KYC pack

    Aadhaar eKYC result, PAN verification result, video-KYC + face-match score.

  2. Penny-drop result + bank statement

    AA-sourced, stored with the consent handle.

  3. Credit bureau report (raw) + score decision

    The pulled report and how it fed the borrower pillar.

  4. Land record extract + income-estimate worksheet + FOIR calc

    From the state portal, with the triangulation shown.

  5. e-NWR verification response + lien-marking confirmation

    Proof the collateral is real and pledged.

  6. Insurance policy + assay certificate + approved-vendor check

    Coverage and quality, from empanelled parties.

  7. Signed agreement + KFS + DPN + NACH mandate + all consent artefacts

    Every one timestamped.

  8. Fraud / negative-check results

    Dedupe, screening, device intelligence.

  9. Six-pillar risk vector at origination + final grade + officer notes

    The decision, reproducible.

  10. Outcome record

    Repaid / late / defaulted / auctioned + recovery %. Populated as the loan closes — this is the calibration label for the whole engine.

What due diligence will challenge — and the answer

Challenge

“The weights and thresholds are arbitrary.”

Response

They are expert-set priors, disclosed as such. Every threshold is a named constant; we recalibrate against our own repayment data. The methods — VaR, CDVI, the shrink formula — are established and citable.

Challenge

“0–100 isn't a probability.”

Response

Correct, and we don't claim it is. It is an ordinal risk index for approve / price / decline decisions. PD calibration comes after we have realised outcomes.

Challenge

“Where's the backtest?”

Response

Not yet — the book is pre-origination. The volatility / VaR pillar can be backtested against historical price drawdowns today; the composite needs loan outcomes. The validation plan and the data it requires are below.

Challenge

“Half the inputs are synthetic.”

Response

Yes — and the product shows which ones, per pillar, via the provenance panel. A real lot scores ~85–97% verified; a demo lot ~20%. We are not hiding it.

Challenge

“The flood-risk table is hardcoded.”

Response

Acknowledged — it is a placeholder. Central Water Commission flood-hazard data is the replacement. It is 10% of a 15%-weight pillar: 1.5% of the composite.

Path from index to validated model

  1. Backtest the volatility / VaR pillar now.

    Historical soybean price data already supports it — compare predicted VaR against realised 90-day drawdowns.

  2. Log every origination.

    Store the six-pillar vector plus the eventual outcome: repaid, margin-called, defaulted, and recovery %.

  3. Refit weights at ~150 closed loans.

    Regularised logistic regression on repaid-vs-defaulted; compare the fitted weights to the expert priors and report the drift.

  4. Calibrate the composite to a real PD curve.

    Publish a reliability diagram so a given score maps to an observed loss rate.

  5. Replace the four data placeholders.

    Flood table → CWC data; district profiles → DES statistics; monsoon series → IMD departures; borrower bands → fitted scorecard.
